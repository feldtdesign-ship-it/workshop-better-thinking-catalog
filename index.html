Section One
Seeing Clearly
Earn the right to reach a conclusion.
9 tools
Seeing ClearlyTool 1.01
Reality Always Wins
Reality has never lost an
argument.

People argue with reality every day. Reality never argues back. It simply waits.

Gravity does not care what we believe. Nature does not care what we intended. Markets do not care what we hoped.

When reality disagrees with us, reality is not the problem. Our understanding is.

The sooner we align ourselves with reality, the sooner our decisions improve.

Before You Decide

  • What do I know?
  • What do I only believe?
  • What evidence supports my conclusion?
  • What evidence challenges it?
Build on this Clean Both Sides of the Glass
Seeing ClearlyTool 1.02
Clean Both Sides of the Glass
To see clearly, you must clean
both sides of the glass.

Every window has two sides. When the view becomes cloudy, most people instinctively clean the side facing everyone else.

Thinking works the same way. We notice flaws in another person's reasoning before recognizing the assumptions and blind spots shaping our own.

Sometimes the distortion belongs to them. Sometimes it belongs to us. Most often, it belongs to both.

Clean your side first. The view usually changes.

Before You Decide

  • What assumptions am I making?
  • Which assumptions have I verified?
  • What facts might I still be missing?
  • What evidence would change my mind?
Build on this If the Facts Disagree with the Theory
Seeing ClearlyTool 1.03
If the Facts Disagree with the Theory
If the facts disagree with the
theory, the theory, not reality,
is wrong.

Ideas deserve respect. Reality deserves authority.

A theory is something to test, not defend. When better evidence appears, change the theory.

Changing your mind when the evidence changes is not weakness. It is intellectual honesty.

Before You Decide

  • Am I testing my conclusion or defending it?
  • What evidence would change my mind?
  • What does reality actually say?
Build on this Collect the Dots Before Connecting Them
Seeing ClearlyTool 1.04
Collect the Dots Before Connecting Them
Collect as many dots as you can
before you start connecting them.

The mind is remarkably good at finding patterns. It is also remarkably good at inventing them.

If something does not make sense, there is a good chance you do not have all the facts yet.

Curiosity gathers. Judgment waits. Resist filling empty spaces with convenient assumptions.

Every conclusion is only as good as the dots you collected before you connected them.

Before You Decide

  • What dots have I collected?
  • Which dots am I assuming?
  • What information is still missing?
  • Am I connecting evidence or filling gaps?
Build on this Never Trust Intuition. Prove It.
Seeing ClearlyTool 1.05
Never Trust Intuition. Prove It.
Never trust your intuition. Prove it.

Intuition is valuable. It often tells us where to begin. It should rarely decide where we end.

A feeling can point toward a possibility. Evidence determines whether that possibility survives reality.

Some of my best ideas began as intuition. Some of my worst mistakes did too. The difference was whether I tested them.

Treat intuition as an invitation to investigate, not permission to conclude.

Before You Decide

  • What evidence supports my intuition?
  • What evidence contradicts it?
  • Am I testing an idea or defending it?
  • What experiment could prove me wrong?
Build on this Bafflegab
Seeing ClearlyTool 1.06
Bafflegab
Language should reduce
uncertainty, not increase it.

Bafflegab creates the illusion of understanding while avoiding genuine explanation. It substitutes unnecessary complexity or fashionable terminology for clear reasoning.

When someone truly understands a subject, they usually make it clearer, not more confusing.

During one particularly complicated meeting, I finally said, “Your bafflegab will certainly lead to a kerfuffle.” Everyone laughed. The point landed.

Sometimes humor explains what argument cannot.

Before You Decide

  • Am I explaining or impressing?
  • Could I make this simpler without making it less true?
  • Do my words reduce uncertainty?
Build on this Better Questions
Seeing ClearlyTool 1.07
Better Questions
The quality of your answers
depends on the quality of your
questions.

Answers often end conversations. Good questions begin them.

When progress stalls, improve the question before searching harder for an answer.

A better question can expose a hidden assumption, reveal a missing fact, or turn an argument into an investigation.

The objective is not endless questioning. It is asking enough of the right questions to deserve the answer.

Before You Decide

  • What problem am I really solving?
  • What question am I avoiding?
  • What would an outsider ask?
  • Have I framed the question too narrowly?
Build on this Remain Teachable
Seeing ClearlyTool 1.08
Remain Teachable
Remain teachable.

Never stop learning is easy to say. Remaining teachable is harder, because it requires humility after experience has made you confident.

Experience is valuable, but it becomes a trap when yesterday's success becomes today's certainty.

The moment you believe there is nothing left to learn, your education quietly stops.

Stay curious enough to search for truth and honest enough to admit when you are still searching.

Before You Decide

  • What can this teach me?
  • What assumption have I stopped questioning?
  • When did experience become certainty?
  • Who knows something I do not?
Build on this Build the Scaffold First
Seeing ClearlyTool 1.09
Build the Scaffold First
Don't climb a ladder to reach
a conclusion. Build a scaffold you
can stand on.

A ladder gets you up quickly. That does not mean you are standing on stable footing.

Too many people decide where they want to land, climb straight toward that conclusion, then build support beneath it after they arrive. They gather facts selectively, defend assumptions, and explain away evidence that does not fit. They are not reasoning. They are retrofitting.

A scaffold works differently. It is built level by level. Each level must be strong enough to support the next.

Observation supports evidence. Evidence tests assumptions. Tested assumptions support reasoning. Reasoning supports the conclusion.

A ladder gives you height. A scaffold gives you stability, guardrails, room to inspect the work, and a wider vantage point. Others can inspect the same structure and test it with you.

Seeing ClearlyTool 1.09 cont.
Build the Scaffold First continued

A conclusion worth keeping should support the weight of evidence, scrutiny, and time. Build the scaffold first.

Before You Decide

  • Am I climbing toward the answer I wanted before I began?
  • What supports this conclusion?
  • Which level of my reasoning is weakest?
  • Did I build support before reaching the conclusion, or after?
  • Could someone else inspect this structure and stand on it with me?

Section Two
Character
Become someone whose thinking can be trusted.
11 tools
CharacterTool 2.01
Accept People as They Come
Accept everyone as they come,
starting with yourself.

Acceptance is not approval.

It is the starting point of honest observation.

You cannot understand a person you are busy trying to rewrite in your head. You cannot improve yourself while pretending you are already someone else.

People arrive with histories, fears, talents, injuries, habits, and blind spots. So do you.

Accepting people as they come does not mean lowering your standards. It means seeing them accurately before deciding what relationship, responsibility, trust, or distance is appropriate.

The same rule applies inward. Self-acceptance is not an excuse to stop growing. It is the place from which honest growth begins.

You cannot build from a false foundation.

Before You Decide

  • Am I seeing this person clearly, or judging who I wish they were?
  • Am I accepting myself honestly, or excusing what I should improve?
  • What boundary, responsibility, or opportunity does reality require?
Build on this Integrity Without an Audience
CharacterTool 2.02
Integrity Without an Audience
Integrity demands doing the
right thing even when no one
is observing.

Integrity is easiest to admire from a distance and hardest to practice in private.

It is not a speech. It is not a reputation. It is the quiet decision to remain aligned with what is right when nobody is there to reward you for it.

A person can perform virtue in public and abandon it in private. That is not integrity. That is theater.

The small choices matter because they become the pattern. Return the borrowed pen. Tell the truth when lying would be convenient. Fix the mistake before anyone notices. Keep the promise after the excitement fades.

Over time, private decisions become public character.

You do not suddenly become trustworthy during great pressure. Pressure reveals whether trustworthiness was already built.

Before You Decide

  • What would I do if no one ever knew?
  • Am I protecting my reputation or my integrity?
  • What small private choice is shaping my character right now?
Build on this Accountability Is Ownership
CharacterTool 2.03
Accountability Is Ownership
If you are not changing something,
you are choosing something.

Accountability begins when excuses end.

That does not mean every problem is your fault. It means your response belongs to you.

Blame is often easier than ownership because blame moves responsibility away from us. Ownership brings it back where action becomes possible.

You are accountable for your decisions. You are also accountable for the opportunities you allow to slip away through delay, avoidance, pride, fear, or distraction.

If something matters and you keep doing nothing, doing nothing has become your choice.

That realization can be uncomfortable. It can also be liberating.

The moment you own your part, you regain the ability to change it.

Before You Decide

  • What part of this situation belongs to me?
  • Am I explaining, avoiding, or owning?
  • What action would accountability require today?
Build on this When You Know Better, Do Better
CharacterTool 2.04
When You Know Better, Do Better
When you know better, you
always do better, unless ego gets
in the way.

Learning is not complete when understanding arrives.

Learning is complete when behavior changes.

A person can collect information for years and remain exactly the same. That is not growth. It is storage.

The hard part is not discovering a better way. The hard part is admitting that the better way now requires something from you.

Once you know better, the old excuse expires.

This is where humility matters. Better knowledge can feel like a gift, but it can also feel like an indictment of how you used to think. Let it be a gift.

You were not wrong forever. You were simply incomplete until reality taught you more.

Before You Decide

  • What do I know now that I did not know before?
  • What behavior should change because of it?
  • Am I resisting the better path because the old one was mine?
Build on this Discipline Determines Whether You Get There
CharacterTool 2.05
Discipline Determines Whether You Get There
Talent defines what you could
become. Motivation gets you
started. Discipline determines
whether you get there.

Talent is useful.

Motivation is useful.

Neither is dependable.

Talent can make the beginning easier, but it often disappears when the work becomes repetitive, difficult, or boring. Motivation can light the match, but it rarely keeps the fire going through weather.

Discipline is different.

Discipline is what remains when the excitement leaves. It is the habit of doing the necessary thing because the goal still matters, not because the mood is favorable.

Build disciplined systems that survive your moods. You know who you are.

The world rarely rewards intensity for a week. It rewards disciplined thinking and disciplined action over time.

Before You Decide

  • Where am I relying on motivation instead of discipline?
  • What system would keep me moving when my mood changes?
  • What small action must be repeated until it becomes reliable?
Build on this Calm Is a Choice
CharacterTool 2.06
Calm Is a Choice
Mad is a choice. So is calm.

Anger often feels automatic.

Sometimes it arrives before we have time to invite it in.

But what happens next is still ours.

I have never seen anyone shout anything done. Shouting may create motion. It rarely creates progress.

Calm does not mean passive. Calm does not mean weak. Calm means you have not handed control of your decisions to the loudest emotion in the room.

Some moments require firmness. Some require immediate action. Some require leaving the room before your mouth creates another problem.

The strongest response is usually the one you can still respect tomorrow.

Before You Decide

  • Am I responding or reacting?
  • What would calm allow me to see that anger is hiding?
  • Will I respect this response tomorrow?
Build on this Pressure Is a Privilege
CharacterTool 2.07
Pressure Is a Privilege
Pressure is a privilege. It means
you are trusted with something
important.

Pressure is uncomfortable because it means the outcome matters.

That is not always bad news.

Pressure often appears when responsibility has found you. Someone trusted you with the problem, the decision, the team, the family, the deadline, the opportunity, or the mission.

Do not focus only on the weight.

Notice the trust.

Pressure can crush people who see only burden. It can strengthen people who understand that responsibility is evidence of importance.

The goal is not to avoid pressure. The goal is to become the kind of person who can carry it without becoming careless, cruel, or afraid.

Treat the pressure of your responsibilities as a privilege.

Before You Decide

  • What responsibility has been trusted to me?
  • Am I focused on the burden or the opportunity?
  • What would steady leadership look like here?
Build on this Recklessness Is Not Courage
CharacterTool 2.08
Recklessness Is Not Courage
No matter how smart, a reckless
person does not learn.

Courage and recklessness can look similar from a distance.

They are not the same.

Courage sees the risk and moves with judgment. Recklessness ignores the risk and calls that bravery.

A simple person can learn. A reckless person often cannot, because recklessness treats consequences as someone else's problem.

Children, and adults who behave like children, should not play with matches.

This is not an argument against bold action. Some situations demand boldness. But boldness without judgment is not leadership. It is a hazard.

Before taking the leap, know whether you are acting from conviction, ego, impatience, or noise.

Before You Decide

  • Am I being courageous or careless?
  • What risk am I pretending not to see?
  • Who pays the price if I am wrong?
Build on this Strength and Kindness Belong Together
CharacterTool 2.09
Strength and Kindness Belong Together
Always be genuine in kindness
or criticism, but never gratuitous
with either.

Kindness without strength can become weakness.

Strength without kindness can become cruelty.

Character requires both.

Be firm in what you say and gentle in how you say it. That is not softness. It is control.

Criticism should serve improvement, not ego. Kindness should be genuine, not performative. Neither should be used as decoration.

Some people confuse harshness with honesty. Others confuse avoidance with compassion. Both are mistakes.

The goal is not to make every conversation comfortable.

The goal is to make it useful, truthful, and humane.

Before You Decide

  • Am I being honest or simply harsh?
  • Am I being kind or avoiding the truth?
  • Can I say this firmly without making it cruel?
Build on this Respect Is Earned
CharacterTool 2.10
Respect Is Earned
Power is recognized. Respect is
earned. You cannot demand either.

Power can be assigned.

Respect cannot.

A title may give someone authority, but it does not automatically create trust. A person may be obeyed because of position and still not be respected because of character.

Respect grows through consistency, fairness, competence, courage, restraint, and how people are treated when they have less power than you do.

To judge someone else's competency or character, you must first display your own.

Demanding respect usually reveals that it has not yet been earned.

Live in a way that makes respect a natural conclusion, not a requested favor.

Before You Decide

  • Am I relying on position or earning trust?
  • How do I treat people who cannot help me?
  • Would my conduct justify the respect I expect?
Build on this Master Yourself First
CharacterTool 2.11
Master Yourself First
Control begins with mastering
yourself: your emotions, actions,
and habits.

Before you can lead a team, repair a system, build a company, or guide a family, you must first learn to govern yourself.

That does not mean perfection.

It means responsibility.

Your emotions are real, but they do not deserve to be in charge of every decision. Your habits are powerful, but they are not destiny. Your impulses may be loud, but volume is not authority.

Self-control is not a cage.

It is the foundation of freedom.

The more you can govern yourself, the less you are governed by distraction, anger, fear, appetite, mood, and other people's opinions.

Master yourself first. Everything else becomes more possible from there.

Before You Decide

  • What currently has more control over me than it should?
  • What habit is quietly shaping my future?
  • What would self-mastery require today?

Relationships

13 tools
RelationshipsTool 3.01
Take People as They Come
Accept everyone as they come,
starting with yourself.

People are not raw material for us to remake into whatever would be most convenient.

Acceptance does not require agreement. It means seeing a person as they actually are before deciding what place, if any, they should have in your life.

Let lions be lions, tigers be tigers, and bears be bears. Lions do not make good bears.

Before You Decide

  • Am I seeing this person as they are, or as I want them to be?
  • Can I accept without surrendering my boundaries?
Build on this Choose Your People Wisely
RelationshipsTool 3.02
Choose Your People Wisely
The people you choose to
be around can change your
entire life.

Proximity is powerful. The people around you influence what becomes normal, what you tolerate, what you attempt, and sometimes what you believe is possible.

Engage with valuable people, not merely available people. Availability is convenient. Value is reciprocal.

Before You Decide

  • Who makes me think better?
  • Who leaves me stronger rather than drained?
  • Am I offering the same value I expect from others?
Build on this Givers Should Water Each Other
RelationshipsTool 3.03
Givers Should Water Each Other
When givers connect,
something rare happens.

Healthy generosity is reciprocal without being transactional.

When two givers connect, neither has to keep score. You water each other. No one is drained. You share the light and both grow.

Generosity should create growth, not dependency.

Before You Decide

  • Is generosity flowing in both directions?
  • Does this relationship replenish or deplete?
Build on this Stand on My Shoulders, Not My Head
RelationshipsTool 3.04
Stand on My Shoulders, Not My Head
Feel free to stand on my
shoulders, but never stand
on my head.

Helping someone rise is one of the privileges of experience.

Share what you know. Open doors. Let someone use your experience as elevation.

But support is not permission for exploitation. Good relationships create elevation without domination.

Before You Decide

  • Am I helping or enabling?
  • Can I be generous without surrendering self-respect?
Build on this Boundaries Need No Apology
RelationshipsTool 3.05
Boundaries Need No Apology
No is a complete decision.

Boundaries are not hostility. They are clarity.

You do not need to write a legal brief every time you say no. Over-explaining often turns a boundary into a negotiation.

Be firm in what you say and gentle in how you say it.

Before You Decide

  • What am I protecting?
  • Have I stated the boundary clearly?
  • Am I explaining because clarity is needed, or because I am seeking permission?
Build on this Believe What People Show You
RelationshipsTool 3.06
Believe What People Show You
When someone shows you
who they are, believe them.

Character is usually revealed in patterns, not speeches.

Pay attention to small things. Someone who returns a borrowed pen without being asked is showing you something. So is someone who casually takes what is not theirs.

Trust should grow from observed behavior over time.

Before You Decide

  • What pattern am I observing?
  • Am I trusting evidence or potential?
  • Has behavior changed, or only the explanation for it?
Build on this Dignity in Disagreement
RelationshipsTool 3.07
Dignity in Disagreement
Disagreement does not require
contempt.

Strong people should be able to challenge an idea without diminishing the person who offered it.

Healthy debate needs friction. It does not need humiliation. Contempt usually makes truth harder to find because people begin defending themselves instead of examining the problem.

Challenge the argument vigorously. Preserve the dignity of the person making it.

Before You Decide

  • Am I attacking the idea or the person?
  • Would I make the same argument without contempt?
  • What matters more: winning the exchange or improving the decision?
Build on this Loyalty Requires Integrity
RelationshipsTool 3.08
Loyalty Requires Integrity
Loyalty is strongest when it
can survive the truth.

Loyalty does not mean defending everything someone does.

Sometimes the most loyal thing you can do is tell someone something they do not want to hear, privately, respectfully, and early enough for it to matter.

Close ranks when necessary, but never close your eyes.

Before You Decide

  • Am I protecting the person or protecting bad behavior?
  • Can this relationship tolerate an honest conversation?
  • Would silence be easier but less loyal?
Build on this Conflict Should Resolve Something
RelationshipsTool 3.09
Conflict Should Resolve Something
I've never seen anyone shout
anything done.

Conflict is sometimes necessary. Chaos rarely is.

Volume is not strength. Anger can make a point louder while making the solution harder to hear.

The purpose of conflict should be clarity, protection, correction, or resolution. Otherwise it is usually just noise.

Before You Decide

  • What outcome am I trying to create?
  • Will what I am about to say improve the situation?
  • Am I responding with purpose or merely reacting?
Build on this The Shards of Vengeance
RelationshipsTool 3.10
The Shards of Vengeance
The shards of vengeance are
sharp on all sides.

Revenge feels directional. Damage rarely is.

Justice, boundaries, and consequences can be necessary. Vengeance is different. It asks pain to heal pain.

Be careful what you break while trying to make someone else bleed.

Before You Decide

  • Do I want resolution, justice, or revenge?
  • What will this action cost me?
  • Will I be freer when this is over?
Build on this Alone Is Not Lonely
RelationshipsTool 3.11
Alone Is Not Lonely
Being alone is not the same as
being lonely.

There is comfort in becoming at home with your own thoughts.

Solitude can restore perspective, creativity, and independence. It teaches you whether you enjoy your own company without requiring you to reject the company of others.

You may choose to stand alone, but never become alone.

Before You Decide

  • Can I be alone without feeling abandoned?
  • Can I belong without surrendering myself?
Build on this Home Should Restore You
RelationshipsTool 3.12
Home Should Restore You
Home is a place to de-stress,
not be stressed.

The world will provide enough friction on its own.

Home should offer some measure of safety, restoration, belonging, and peace. That does not mean a home without disagreement. It means a place where disagreement does not destroy security.

Our favorite places are often where our favorite people are.

Before You Decide

  • Does my home restore me?
  • Do the people I love feel safe being themselves there?
  • What could I change to make home more peaceful?
Build on this Let Them Find Their Own Path
RelationshipsTool 3.13
Let Them Find Their Own Path
Love people enough to let their life
belong to them.

Parenting, mentoring, and leadership all contain the same temptation: confusing guidance with ownership.

Give people what you can. Teach them. Protect them when protection is appropriate. Share your mistakes. Open doors. Then let them walk.

Success is not creating smaller versions of ourselves. It is helping people develop enough confidence, judgment, and courage to choose their own path.

Before You Decide

  • Am I guiding or controlling?
  • Have I made room for a path I would not choose?
  • Can I be proud without needing credit?

Influence

11 tools
InfluenceTool 4.01
Nobody Wins Unless Everybody Wins
There is not just one winner on
a real team.

A team victory that leaves people diminished is not really a victory. It is a transaction with damage hidden inside it.

The strongest outcomes create shared momentum, shared ownership, and shared pride.

No one wins unless everyone wins does not mean everyone gets everything they want. It means the mission is designed so that success strengthens the whole system.

Before You Influence

  • Who is being left behind by this version of success?
  • Does this outcome strengthen the system or merely reward one part of it?
Build on this Force Multipliers
InfluenceTool 4.02
Force Multipliers
Those who make things happen
are a force. Those who lead them
are force multipliers.

A force can accomplish a great deal. A force multiplier creates alignment, direction, resources, confidence, and momentum so many capable people can accomplish what no single person could accomplish alone.

The goal is not to remain the strongest person in the room. It is to increase the strength of the room.

Before You Influence

  • Who becomes more capable because I am here?
  • Am I solving everything myself or multiplying capability?
Build on this Great Ideas Are Rarely Your Own
InfluenceTool 4.03
Great Ideas Are Rarely Your Own
Successful people understand
that great ideas are not always
their own.

Listen for the idea, not merely the title of the person offering it.

Some of the best improvements come from someone close enough to the work to see what leadership missed.

If you want people to keep bringing you their best thinking, make sure they recognize themselves in the success.

Before You Influence

  • Whose thinking improved this work?
  • Have I confused leadership with authorship?
Build on this Purpose Before Ego
InfluenceTool 4.04
Purpose Before Ego
Lead by four cornerstones:
people before ego,
purpose before noise, proof
before opinion, pace before
perfection.

Ego distorts influence. Noise dilutes purpose. Opinion without proof weakens trust. Perfection can slow work that needs progress.

These four cornerstones are not decorative. They are guardrails for influence under pressure.

Before You Influence

  • Which cornerstone is weakest here?
  • Am I protecting purpose or protecting ego?
Build on this Firm Words, Gentle Delivery
InfluenceTool 4.05
Firm Words, Gentle Delivery
Be firm in what you say and
gentle in how you say it.

Clarity does not require cruelty. Gentleness does not require weakness.

The best communication often combines a steady position with enough respect that the other person can still hear it.

Tone can either carry truth or bury it under resistance.

Before You Influence

  • Is my message clear?
  • Can I say it more gently without weakening it?
  • Am I trying to solve or merely strike?
Build on this One Warning Is Powerful
InfluenceTool 4.06
One Warning Is Powerful
One warning is powerful.
Multiple warnings become
empty threats.

A clear warning draws a boundary and gives the other person a chance to respond.

Repeated warnings without action teach people that your boundary is negotiable.

Say what matters once. Then act deliberately if the line is crossed.

Before You Influence

  • Have I already made the boundary clear?
  • What action follows if nothing changes?
Build on this Let the Room Come to You
InfluenceTool 4.07
Let the Room Come to You
Let the room come to you
before speaking.

Influence is not always improved by being first.

A room gives information before it gives permission. Watch posture, incentives, tension, confusion, and who is not speaking.

Sometimes the strongest contribution comes after the room has revealed what it actually needs.

Before You Influence

  • What is the room telling me?
  • Who has not spoken?
  • Would waiting improve the quality of my contribution?
Build on this Willful Ignorance
InfluenceTool 4.08
Willful Ignorance Is Dangerous
Willful ignorance becomes
dangerous when the
person refusing to listen is
empowered to decide.

Not knowing is human. Refusing to know is different.

Decision makers who cover their ears while allocating resources, setting policy, or directing people can turn ignorance into harm.

Influence sometimes requires insisting that facts enter the room.

Before You Influence

  • What information is being avoided?
  • Who is empowered to decide without understanding?
  • How can facts be made harder to ignore?
Build on this The Hard Stuff Is Why You Are Here
InfluenceTool 4.09
The Hard Stuff Is Why You Are Here
If what we do here was easy,
someone else would be
sitting in your chair.

Pressure often means you have been trusted with something important.

Do not waste the moment wishing the work were easier. Focus on the opportunity to prove the team capable of doing difficult things well.

The hard stuff is often where trust is built.

Before You Influence

  • What responsibility has been entrusted to me?
  • What does this pressure make possible?
  • How can I help the team see the opportunity?
Build on this No Miracle People
InfluenceTool 4.10
No Miracle People

Do work that leaves future people with more options, healthier systems, clearer information, and fewer hidden problems.

You do not have to leave everything perfect.

Leave it capable of becoming better.

Before You Act

  • What condition did I inherit?
  • What condition will I leave?
  • Will the next steward have better information because I was here?
  • Will the system be more capable of healing after I leave?

I don't want disciples. I want

Disciples preserve ideas. Successors improve them.

LegacyTool 9.09
Let Them Own the Work
The work has not transferred until
someone else feels ownership.

Delegation moves tasks. Succession transfers responsibility.

People rarely protect work they feel they are merely borrowing. Give successors enough authority to make decisions, enough context to understand consequences, and enough room to make the work their own.

They will do some things differently. That is not automatically failure. It may be evidence that succession actually occurred.

If you require permanent control, you have not built succession.
